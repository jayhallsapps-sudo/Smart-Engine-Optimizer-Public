# Local SEO for Behavioral Health 101

This document explains how Webserv uses location pages to establish geographic trust without creating city-based SEO risk.

---

## Core Philosophy

Local SEO for behavioral health is **not** about ranking for every city or creating dozens of geo pages. It is about establishing clear geographic legitimacy for real treatment facilities so Google and users can confidently understand:
- Where care is physically delivered
- Which services are available at which locations
- How the organization is structured across locations

**In regulated, YMYL healthcare spaces, clarity and legitimacy outperform scale.**

---

## The Role Local SEO Plays in the Treatment Center SEO Priority Framework

Local SEO lives primarily in **Tier 2** of the Treatment Center SEO Priority Framework:
- It supports Tier 1 eligibility pages
- It prevents fragmentation and doorway risk in Tier 3

> Think of Tier 2 Location Pages as structural authority. Local SEO does not create demand or eligibility on its own. Instead, it anchors geographic trust so Tier 1 service and modality pages can perform.

---

## What Local SEO Is NOT

Local SEO is not:
- A strategy for spinning up city-name variants
- A replacement for service or modality pages
- A volume play

Those approaches worked pre-Medic. **They now introduce risk.**

---

## What a "Primary Location Page" Is

A Primary Location Page represents:
- One real, physical address
- One distinct treatment facility
- A canonical geographic anchor for that location

Each real address can justify one Primary Location Page. Examples:
- Residential Rehab Facility – Address A
- Detox Facility – Address B
- Sober Living Home – Address C

Each gets **one authoritative page, not multiple city variants.**

### What These Pages Do Strategically
- Consolidate all geographic relevance for a facility
- Serve as the internal linking hub for services offered at that location
- Reinforce legitimacy for Google Business Profiles
- Reduce cannibalization between service + geo intent

### What These Pages Are NOT
- "Rehab in [City]" SEO pages
- Catch-alls for ranking in nearby towns
- Thin templates with swapped city names

> If a page exists only to rank and does not represent a real location, it does not belong. Proximity is not a location. Addresses are.

## Common Client Request: "We're Close To [City] — Can We Add a Page?"

Clients will frequently ask for pages like:
- "We're 15 minutes from Malibu"
- "We're just over the hill from Santa Monica"
- "People in [Nearby City] come to us all the time"

This is a normal request, and it usually comes from good intent.

### How We Evaluate This Request
**One simple rule: If there is no physical treatment facility at that address, we do not create a location page for it.**

Driving distance, proximity, bridges, hills, or "service area" logic do not change this rule.

### Why We Don't Create Those Pages
Pages for cities without real facilities:
- Are easily interpreted as doorway pages
- Fragment geographic authority
- Create long-term indexation and ranking instability
- Undermine trust in a regulated healthcare space

Even when they work temporarily, they introduce risk that compounds over time.

### What We Do Instead
Handle nearby city demand **inside the real location page**, not as a new page.

Acceptable ways to address proximity:
- Brief copy: "Located in [City A], serving clients from [Nearby City B] and surrounding areas."
- Clear directions or travel context on the real location page
- Admissions or FAQ language that acknowledges where clients commonly travel from

This preserves clarity without creating SEO risk.

### Trusted Advisor Response to This Request

1. **Acknowledge & Validate**: "You're absolutely right — proximity does matter, and it's very common for people searching in Malibu to end up choosing facilities nearby."

2. **Set a Clear Expectation**: "The challenge isn't distance — it's whether Google can verify that care is actually delivered at that location. If there's no physical treatment facility there, creating a standalone page puts the site at risk."

3. **Reframe the Strategy**: "Instead of creating a separate Malibu page, we handle that demand inside your real location page so it strengthens rankings rather than fragments them."

4. **Explain What We'll Do Instead**: "We'll reinforce proximity directly on the location page — through copy, directions, and admissions language — so Google and users clearly understand that clients from Malibu regularly come to this facility."

5. **Close With Reassurance**: "This approach protects the site long term, avoids risk, and still captures demand from nearby cities — without introducing pages that could destabilize rankings later."

## Information Architecture & Navigation Placement

Primary Location Pages should be:
- Discoverable, but not dominant
- Clearly separated from services and modalities
- Grouped logically under a Locations or Our Locations parent

### Recommended Navigation Structure
```
About
Programs / Services
Conditions We Treat
Locations
Verify Insurance
Contact
```

### Recommended URL Structure
```
/locations/
/locations/los-angeles-rehab/
/locations/san-diego-detox/
/locations/orange-county-sober-living/
```

### What NOT to Do in Information Architecture
- Putting location pages under /services/
- Putting location pages under the root
- Mixing city pages with modalities
- Creating multiple pages for nearby cities where no address is present
- Over-exposing locations in the main nav when there is only one facility

---

## What "Good" Looks Like

A strong Local SEO setup for behavioral health:
- One authoritative page per real address
- Clear differentiation between: Services (what care) and Locations (where care)
- Strong internal linking **to** location pages, not excessive linking **from** them
- Location pages that feel legitimate to users first, search engines second

When done correctly:
- Google understands the entity
- Users trust the organization
- Tier 1 pages rank more reliably
- Indexation improves across the site

---

## What We Explicitly Avoid (and Why)

### City Variant Pages Without Addresses
Risk: Doorway page classification, indexing suppression, cannibalization with service pages

### Templated Location Pages at Scale
Risk: "Discovered – currently not indexed," weak engagement signals, loss of trust in YMYL space

### Using Location Pages as Lead-Gen Substitutes
Risk: Confused intent, poor conversion quality, structural SEO breakdown

**Location pages support conversion — they should not replace eligibility pages.**

---

## How We Measure Success

Local SEO success is **not** measured by how many city keywords a location page ranks for.

Primary Location Pages are structural assets, not traffic traps. Their success is measured by how effectively they clarify geographic legitimacy and enable other pages to perform.

> TL;DR: If location pages are ranking **instead** of service pages, something is wrong. If service pages can rank locally without being city pages, the structure is working.

### Core Health Signals
- Service pages ranking for local queries (not location pages)
- GBP profile properly linked and verified
- No location pages competing with service pages
- Consistent NAP across all pages and GBP

### Secondary Health Signals
- Location pages indexed and not in "Discovered – not indexed" state
- Internal linking from service pages to location pages is working
- No cannibalization between location and service URLs
