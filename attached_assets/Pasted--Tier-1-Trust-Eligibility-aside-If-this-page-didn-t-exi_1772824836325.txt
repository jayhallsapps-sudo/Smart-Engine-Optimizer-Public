# **Tier 1 — Trust & Eligibility**

<aside>
📌

*If this page didn’t exist or was unclear, could Google or a user misunderstand what the center does or how to actually get help?*

Tier 1 is not just about services — it’s about **eligibility and clarity.**

If a core page is missing, weak, or cannibalized, **it gets priority over everything else**.

A treatment center is not fully eligible (to users or to Google) if:

- There is no clear way to initiate care
- The admissions pathway is ambiguous
- High-intent users are forced into indirect or misleading flows

In a YMYL healthcare context, **access to care is part of trust.**

If a core page that defines services or access is missing, weak, or cannibalized, **it gets priority over everything else.**

If yes → **Priority 1**

</aside>

## **Why this tier comes first**

Since the Medic update and reinforced by Helpful Content, Google:

- Chooses *one* primary URL per service intent
- Suppresses sites with fragmented service pages

No amount of blogs or condition pages will overcome weak service authority

### **Examples :**

- Specialized Modality: Detox Services
    - Explains if detox is medically supervised
    - Clarifies monitoring, safety and withdrawal support
    - On-site or off site
    - Not substance-specific
- Primary Modalities (Levles of Care)
    - Residential / Inpatient
    - PHP /IOP
    - Sober Living
- Dual Diagnosis
    - Clarifies integrated care vs. mental health only
    - Program-level explanation, not condition-specific
- Access to care
    - Contact Us
    - Verify insurance

### **How to scope:**

New: 

- **Service (2 credits):** Modality Pages, Dual Diagnosis, Detox
- **Signature (2 credits):** Access to care

Refrehes: 

- **Scale (1 credit)** : Modality Pages, Dual Diagnosis, Access to Care
- **CRO Work (0 credit):** Light updates (less than an hour) on Access to Care or Modality pages

# **Tier 2 — Structural Authority**

<aside>
📌

*If this page didn’t exist, would the site feel disorganized or harder to understand? Is this a page many other pages should point to, not just land on?*

Once trust and eligibility are established, Google needs to understand **how the site is organized** and which pages act as structural anchors.

If yes → **Priority 2**

</aside>

## **Why this tier comes second**

- Structural pages determine how authority flows internally
- Many other pages depend on these hubs to make sense
- Poor structure causes even strong pages to underperform

Without clear structural nodes, new content competes instead of compounding.

### **Examples:**

- **Conditions hub (What We Treat)**
    - Top-level index of conditions
    - Links out to condition pages
    - Does *not* act as a service page
- **Therapies / Modalities hub**
    - Overview of treatment approaches
    - Links to individual therapy pages
    - Supports credibility, not conversion
- **Primary location page**
    - Defines physical location and service area
    - Acts as a geographic anchor for the site

### **How to scope:**

New

- **Service (2 credits):** Conditions, Therapies
- **Signature (2 credits):** Location pages

Refrehes: 

- **Scale (1 credit)** : Modality Pages, Dual Diagnosis, Access to Care

# **Tier 3 — Consolidation & Cleanup**

<aside>
📌

*Does this reduce URL sprawl or clean up past SEO decisions?*

Before adding more pages, the site must stop **working against itself**.

If yes → **Priority 3**

</aside>

## **Why this tier comes third**

- Legacy SEO decisions often create internal competition
- Redundant pages dilute authority and confuse intent
- Cleanup increases the impact of every future page

Consolidation ensures growth is additive, not offset by structural drag.

### **Examples:**

**Duplicate / Competing Service URLs:** 

- Multiple “rehab” or “detox” service variations consolidated into one primary service page
    
    *(e.g., rehab-near-me, rehab-centers, rehab-programs → Residential Treatment)*
    
- Substance-agnostic service pages replacing thin keyword variants
    
    *(e.g., drug-rehab-facilities, drug-rehab-clinics → Drug Rehab)*
    

**Legacy Location & “Near Me” Pages:** 

- City, county, or “near me” pages retired and redirected into the primary location or service page
    
    *(e.g., detox-near-me, rehab-in-[city] → Detox Services / Residential Treatment)*
    
- Overlapping geo pages consolidated into a single geographic anchor
    
    *(e.g., multiple Ventura / Ojai / Malibu rehab pages → one primary location page)*
    

**Taxonomy, Tag & Low-Value Archive Cleanup:** 

- Tag or category archive pages removed or noindexed
    
    *(e.g., /tag/alcohol-rehab/, /tag/drug-detox/)*
    
- Old blog posts or thin resources merged into a stronger, current page
    
    *(e.g., multiple “what is detox” posts → one updated overview)*
    

### **How to scope:**

- **0 credits:** Scoped as part of technical SEO, not content production
- Should generally require **< 2 hours per month** to maintain and clean up legacy issues as part of regular in scope technical seo support

# **Tier 4 — Conversion & Differentiation**

<aside>
📌

*Would this page meaningfully influence a person’s decision to choose this center, once they already understand the level of care offered?*

Only after clarity and structure are in place does **differentiation actually matter.**

If yes → **Priority 4**

</aside>

## **Why this tier comes fourth**

This tier comes fourth because:

- At this stage, users are comparing options
- Google already understands what the center does
- These pages influence *choice*, not eligibility

In Tier 4, conversion improvements are a byproduct of differentiation and confidence-building — not standalone CRO experiments.

### Examples:

- About
    - Meet the team / Clinical leadership
    - Staff detail pages
    - Aftercare & Alumni support

### **How to scope:**

New

- **Signature (2 credits):** New differentiation or conversion-focused pages

Refrehes: 

- **Scale (1 credit)** : Serious updates or optimizations to existing Tier 4 pages
- **CRO Work (0 credit):** Light updates (less than an hour)

# **Tier 5 — Expansion & Demand Capture**

<aside>
📌

*Does this assume the site is already trusted and clear? Is this about traffic growth rather than clarity or structure?* 

Traffic expansion assumes the site is already trusted, clear, and well-structured.

Demand capture should amplify a strong foundation, not attempt to replace it.

If yes → **Priority 5**

</aside>

## **Why this tier comes last**

This tier comes last because:

- Blogs and informational content cannot compensate for structural weaknesses
- Early expansion creates bloat and misalignment
- Google now rewards usefulness *after* clarity

### Examples:

- Blogs
- FAQs
- High-volume informational content
- Long-tail education pieces
- Quiz / Infographic
- live in a /blog/ or /category-name/ subfolder

### **How to scope:**

New 

- **Scale (1 credit):** Standard blogs and supporting content
- **Signature (2 credits, rare):** Long-tail, in-depth educational assets when intentionally planned

Refresh: 

- **Scale (1 credit):** Important blog that requires a full refresh (ranking 30+ or not ranking for target keyword
- **Typical Optimization (0.5 credits)**: Ranking 30+, requires edits and minor structural changes
