# The Art of Client Service: Managing Client Expectations

## Why Expectations Matter More Than Performance

- Mismanaged expectations = perceived failure (even with good outcomes)
- Clear expectations = trust, patience, and partnership
- Surprises erode confidence faster than bad news

> Clients don't judge us only by results. They judge us by whether reality matches what they expected.

---

## Expectation Management Is a Leadership Skill

- Clients don't want vendors, they want advisors
- Advisors help clients understand trade-offs and constraints
- Clients buy outcomes, not activities
- Your job is to guide them through reality — not validate assumptions

---

## The 3 Types of Expectations You Must Manage

1. **Outcome Expectations** — What success actually looks like
2. **Process Expectations** — How work gets done (timelines, inputs, reviews)
3. **Relationship Expectations** — How communication, ownership, and escalation work

---

## The #1 Expectation Failure: Implicit Assumptions

**Common Traps**:
- "They know how this works"
- "We talked about this once"
- "It's implied"

> If it's not documented, it's not agreed. Verbal alignment ≠ shared understanding.

---

## Replace Assumptions With Explicit Agreements

| Best Practice | Language to Model |
|---------------|------------------|
| Turn assumptions into written plans | "Let's align on what success looks like" |
| Define success criteria | "Here's what we can control vs. what we can't" |
| Agree on timelines and responsibilities | "Here's what happens if X slips" |

---

## The Trusted Advisor Mindset: Saying No Without Losing Trust

> Saying yes to everything is not service. It's abdication of responsibility.
> Good advisors protect clients from bad decisions. Even when those decisions are requested.

### How to Reframe "No" as Leadership

**Structure**:
1. Acknowledge the goal
2. Explain the constraint
3. Offer a viable alternative

**Example**:
> "I understand why you want this outcome. Given the timeline and data we have, this approach won't get us there. Here's what will move us forward."

---

## Expectations Are Ongoing, Not One-Time

Alignment decays. Context fades. Teams change.

**Best Practice**: Re-anchor expectations regularly, especially at:
- Monthly reviews
- Scope changes
- Performance dips

---

## When Expectations MUST Be Set — A Simple Matrix

Mandatory Expectation Setting Moments:
- Onboarding / kickoff
- Strategy changes
- Budget changes
- Performance inflection points (up or down)
- Before QBRs (not during)
- After missed timelines or dependencies

---

## Managing Expectations During Change

**Reality**: Strategy shifts. Markets change. Priorities evolve.

> Re-set expectations at the **moment of change**, not after impact.

---

## The Power of Narrative Updates

**Bad Update**: "We're behind."

**Good Update**:
- What changed
- Why it matters
- What's being done
- What to expect next

---

## Warning Signs Expectations Are Slipping

- Vague dissatisfaction
- "Just checking in" emails
- Sudden urgency
- Shifting success definitions

---

## What Great Client Service Does

1. Makes the goal measurable
2. Names the constraints early
3. Documents the plan + owners
4. Flags risks before they hurt
5. Resets expectations fast when reality changes

---

## Expectation Management Is Part of Performance Reviews

AM performance is judged on:
- Clarity of expectations set
- Quality of documentation
- Proactive risk flagging
- Client surprise rate (zero is the goal)
- Ability to say no with context

---

## Final Takeaway

> Your job isn't [just] to manage accounts. It's to manage expectations so results can be evaluated fairly.
