Refactor the existing QBR Prep “Additional Opportunities” implementation so it follows the intended strategy and fixes the current classification problems.

Primary objective

The Additional Opportunities section must only surface justified upsells or cross-sells after the full QBR Prep report is assembled and evaluated.

The section must:
- filter standard SEO scope issues out first
- only escalate when justified
- separate upsell vs cross-sell cleanly

- avoid filler

Critical fixes required

Fix 1 — Conversion tracking / infrastructure cannot be treated as an upsell by default

Right now, “Conversion Infrastructure & Tracking Setup” is being surfaced as an upsell. That is wrong in the default case.

Missing or weak conversion tracking should generally be treated as a foundational recommendation inside standard scope or standard report recommendations, not as an Additional Opportunity.

Only allow a tracking-related Additional Opportunity if the recommendation is clearly being packaged as a distinct paid analytics / CRO implementation project outside the normal SEO roadmap. If that level of packaging is not clearly supported in the report, do not surface it in Additional Opportunities.

Fix 2 — Expand trigger coverage beyond the current hard-coded set

The current implementation is too narrow. It needs broader coverage so legitimate opportunities are not missed.

The logic must support the full intended categories below:

Potential standard-scope issue patterns:
- flat or declining organic traffic
- rankings but weak admissions / weak conversion
- over-reliance on branded search
- low organic CTR
- high bounce / low engagement
- strong content with low visibility
- limited local visibility
- content not driving leads
- technical SEO holding growth back
- competitive market / saturated SERP environment
- organic traffic not converting by location
- authority gap vs competitors

Potential upsell patterns:
- need for deeper strategic coverage
- brand authority / signature content opportunity
- early traction or flat traffic caused by low content volume
- strong traffic with low conversion where landing page build / CRO support is beyond standard roadmap
- custom authority-building project such as digital PR / selective link acquisition when clearly justified

Potential cross-sell patterns:
- flat organic growth despite rankings where paid media can test and scale faster
- high-ranking pages with no local visibility where local paid search could drive direct admits
- strong performing blog / SEO content where retargeting, audience building, or content boosting makes sense
- organic CTR weakness where search ads could protect or capture demand
- content production outpacing visibility where paid social / content promotion fits

Fix 3 — Build a true “standard scope first” classification layer

The current implementation appears to jump from a small set of signals directly into upsell or cross-sell decisions.

That must change.

Every major gap must go through a three-stage model:

Stage 1: standard scope vs escalation candidate
Stage 2: if escalation candidate, determine upsell vs cross-sell
Stage 3: score and rank opportunities

No issue should become an Additional Opportunity unless it first fails the standard-scope test.

Required Stage 1 logic

For every meaningful report gap, ask:

1. Is this issue already reasonably solvable inside normal SEO scope?
Examples:
- service page gaps
- CTA / internal link / trust signal weaknesses
- local SEO improvements
- technical cleanup
- metadata / CTR work
- content refreshes
- blog-to-service journey fixes
- geo-page improvements
- normal roadmap content planning

If yes, keep it out of Additional Opportunities unless there is strong evidence that ordinary scope is insufficient.

2. Is the issue tied to a business outcome?
Valid ties include:
- admits
- VOBs
- qualified calls
- lead quality
- conversion quality
- local acquisition
- meaningful demand capture
- meaningful growth acceleration

Traffic-only logic is not enough.

3. Is there enough report evidence to justify escalation?
Do not escalate weak, vague, or incomplete findings.

Only if the issue is:
- business-relevant
- evidence-backed
- not adequately handled by standard scope alone

…can it move to Stage 2.

Fix 4 — Remove circular dependency on Section 6 or other specific section phrasing

The Additional Opportunities generator must evaluate the final report data directly.

Do not require an opportunity to already appear in Section 6 or any other section before it can be surfaced here.

Examples of bad logic that must be removed:
- content upsell only firing if a content initiative already exists in S6
- opportunity logic depending on wording from another section instead of underlying report evidence

The post-processing pass must inspect report facts, not prior phrasing.

Fix 5 — Add an actual scoring and ranking system

The current summary describes fire conditions, but not a real ranking system. That is not enough.

If multiple valid opportunities exist, score each candidate across the following dimensions:

1. Business impact
How directly could this improve admits, qualified leads, VOBs, conversion quality, or local acquisition?

2. Evidence strength
How clearly is the opportunity supported by the report data?

3. Urgency
How time-sensitive is the opportunity based on pace to goal, missed demand, or current quarter risk?

4. Strategic fit
How well does the opportunity align with the client’s current goals, tier, account state, and report narrative?

Use a transparent weighted score.

Recommended default weighting:
- business impact: 40%
- evidence strength: 30%
- urgency: 20%
- strategic fit: 10%

Return only the top 1 to 3 highest-scoring opportunities that pass the minimum threshold.

Minimum threshold requirement

Do not output an Additional Opportunity unless:
- business impact score is meaningful
- evidence score is meaningful
- total weighted score crosses a reasonable minimum threshold

If nothing crosses the threshold, return no additional opportunities.

Required classification framework

Use the following framework exactly.

STANDARD SCOPE
An issue belongs in standard scope when it should reasonably be addressed through the normal SEO roadmap:
- content refreshes
- service page improvements
- CTA improvements
- internal linking
- technical fixes
- local SEO improvements
- metadata and CTR optimization
- geo-page strategy
- blog-to-conversion path improvements
- normal CRO recommendations
- normal content planning

Do not surface these in Additional Opportunities unless there is strong evidence that standard scope is insufficient and additional investment is needed to materially accelerate results.

UPSELL
An issue becomes an upsell only when SEO is still the right lever, but the current scope is too limited in depth, scale, specialization, or speed.

Valid upsell classes:
- Expanded Service / Funnel Coverage
- Signature / Authority Content
- Expanded Content Production
- Advanced Landing Page / CRO Project
- Custom Authority-Building Project

Important:
Tracking / instrumentation alone is not an upsell by default.

CROSS_SELL
An issue becomes a cross-sell only when another channel outside SEO is the more direct acceleration lever.

Valid cross-sell classes:
- Paid Search Demand Capture
- Local Paid Search
- Retargeting / Audience Building
- Search Ads Protection / Demand Capture
- Paid Social / Content Promotion

No-opportunity rules

Return no Additional Opportunity when any of the following is true:
- the issue is already adequately addressed by standard SEO scope
- the evidence is weak
- the issue has no clear business tie
- the recommendation would read like a forced sales pitch
- the opportunity is really just a basic housekeeping or tracking prerequisite
- there is not enough confirmed report data to cite

Tone requirements

Use brief, consultative, evidence-based language.

Good tone:
- optional acceleration lever
- if the client wants to move faster
- based on the current performance pattern
- supported by the current report findings

Bad tone:
- hard sell
- deficiency framing
- vague growth fluff
- traffic-only reasoning
- aggressive sales copy

Special handling rules

Rule A — tracking gaps
Missing conversion tracking should usually stay in standard recommendations, not Additional Opportunities.

Rule B — authority-building
Authority-building can be an upsell when there is a confirmed authority gap vs competitors and normal scope is not enough.

Rule C — content expansion
Expanded content production can be an upsell when report data confirms multiple high-intent opportunities and current production depth is too limited. Do not make it depend on Section 6 phrasing.

Rule D — paid search
Paid Search can be a cross-sell when the report shows meaningful demand and quarter risk, and another channel would close the gap faster than organic alone.

Rule E — retargeting / promotion
Retargeting or paid promotion can be a cross-sell when content engagement exists but conversion capture or distribution is lagging.
